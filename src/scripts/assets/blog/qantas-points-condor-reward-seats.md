Qantas Frequent Flyer has added a new airline partner, and this one opens up a stretch of Europe that was previously hard to reach on points. As of September 1, 2026, members can redeem Qantas Points for Classic Reward seats on Condor, the Frankfurt-based leisure airline known for its beach-towel striped aircraft. The addition pushes the Qantas program past 30 airline partners and roughly 1,300 destinations, and it puts hundreds of thousands of new reward seats across Europe, the Mediterranean, North America, and the Caribbean within reach of a points balance.

If you have never flown Qantas and never plan to, this still matters. Qantas Points are reachable in the United States through several transferable card currencies, which means a Condor business-class seat to Frankfurt can be booked with points you earned on everyday spending. Below is what the partnership actually delivers, what it costs, and the one discipline that keeps you from wasting points on it.

## What actually changed

The mechanics are simple. Qantas Frequent Flyer members can now **use** their points on flights with a Condor flight number. You cannot **earn** Qantas Points by flying Condor, only spend them, which is the normal shape of a redemption-only partnership. Seats are bookable across three cabins: Economy, Premium Economy, and Business.

Condor sits on the Qantas Partner Classic Flight Rewards table, which means its awards are priced by distance band and cabin, the same structure Qantas uses for its other partner airlines. The seats show up in the standard Qantas award search, and you book them the way you would any other Classic Reward. There is nothing exotic about the process once you know the partner exists.

One practical detail from the announcement: a fresh interline agreement lets baggage check straight through to your final destination when a Qantas and Condor journey is booked on a single ticket. That makes a connecting itinerary across the two networks far less painful than two separate bookings stapled together.

## Who Condor is, and what you are booking

Condor is Germany's other long-haul carrier, the one that is not Lufthansa. It leans toward leisure and holiday routes, flying to more than 70 destinations across Europe, the Mediterranean, North America, Central America, and the Caribbean. The livery is hard to miss, with candy stripes inspired by beach towels and deck chairs.

The part worth your attention is the long-haul fleet. Condor's newest widebodies are Airbus A330neo aircraft fitted with 30 lie-flat Business seats in a 1-2-1 layout, so every business passenger gets direct aisle access. The hardware is built on Safran's Skylounge Core platform, the same underlying seat family used in some other well-regarded business cabins. The front row adds a handful of upgraded Business Prime berths with more space and a larger screen. Reviews of the business product are generally positive, which is the cabin most points travelers will be chasing here.

Be more careful with Premium Economy. Frequent-flyer commentary on Condor's premium economy is mixed, with several travelers describing it as an extra-legroom economy seat rather than a true premium cabin. If you are weighing Premium Economy against Economy on points, treat it as a modest comfort upgrade rather than a step-change, and price the difference accordingly.

## What it costs in points

Because Condor is priced off the partner distance-band table, the point cost depends on the segment length, not the specific city pair. Here are sample published rates at the time of writing, each **plus taxes, fees, and carrier charges**:

- **Bangkok to Frankfurt**, Business: 108,000 points
- **Bangkok to Frankfurt**, Premium Economy: 85,000 points
- **Bangkok to Frankfurt**, Economy: 43,500 points
- **Los Angeles to Frankfurt**, Economy: 51,800 points
- **Frankfurt to Cancun**, Economy: 43,500 points
- **Frankfurt to London Gatwick**, Business: 21,000 points
- **Frankfurt to Ibiza or Corfu**, Economy: 16,100 points

A long-haul Condor business seat in the 100,000-point range is a solid, not spectacular, partner rate, and it is often easier to find than an equivalent seat on Qantas metal. The short intra-Europe hops are where the chart gets interesting: a 16,100-point economy seat to a Mediterranean island, or a 21,000-point business hop from Frankfurt to London, can be excellent value as a positioning flight or a short getaway.

The phrase to keep in mind is "plus taxes, fees, and carrier charges." Partner awards routed through Europe can carry meaningful surcharges, and a low headline point cost can hide a cash component that changes the math. Always read the full price, points and cash together, before you decide a redemption is a good deal. We wrote about this trap in [how to avoid fuel surcharges on award flights](/blog/avoid-fuel-surcharges-award-flights), and it applies squarely here.

## The routes that make sense

Condor does not fly to Australia or New Zealand, so no one is boarding a Condor jet at home. Every itinerary is a connection: you get yourself to one of Condor's gateways, then pick up the Condor flight onward. The Qantas and Condor networks overlap at a useful set of hubs, including Bangkok, Los Angeles, San Francisco, New York, Vancouver, Johannesburg, Paris, and Rome.

For a reader earning points in the United States, the most natural pattern is a US gateway into Frankfurt, then Condor's European and Mediterranean network from there. Los Angeles, San Francisco, and New York all connect to Frankfurt, and from Frankfurt the short-haul chart opens up dozens of leisure destinations at low point costs. That combination, a transatlantic long-haul plus a cheap intra-Europe hop, is where this partnership earns its place in a points strategy.

The limitation is Asia. Condor's network into Asia is thin, so travelers routing through Bangkok will find fewer onward options than the Frankfurt hub offers. If your trip is Europe-shaped, Condor is a genuine new tool. If it is Asia-shaped, it changes little.

## How to book a Condor reward seat

The process is the same as any Qantas Classic Reward, with one habit that protects your points.

1. Search award space in the Qantas booking tool or the Qantas Flight Reward Finder, and look for the red Reward ribbon that marks a Classic Reward seat.
2. Confirm the specific Condor segment you want is genuinely available, in the cabin and on the dates you need, not just displayed.
3. Check the full price: the point cost **and** the taxes, fees, and carrier charges attached to it.
4. If you are transferring points in to make the booking, transfer only after the seat is confirmed, then book immediately.

That fourth step is the one people skip, and it is the one that costs them. Which brings us to where US points fit.

## Where United States points come in

You do not need a Qantas-branded credit card to build a Qantas balance. In the United States, several transferable point programs feed Qantas Frequent Flyer, including American Express Membership Rewards, Citi ThankYou, Capital One Miles, and Bilt Rewards. That means Qantas Points, and now Condor seats, are reachable from the same flexible balances you would use for any other partner.

This is exactly the situation our [guide to transfer partners](/blog/transfer-partners-explained-guide) was written for, and the golden rule from that guide is non-negotiable here: **find and confirm the Condor seat first, then transfer.** Transfers into Qantas are effectively one-way. If you move points speculatively because a Condor business seat *looks* available, and the space is gone by the time your transfer posts, those points are stranded in Qantas with no path back to your card. Confirm the seat, verify your card's exact transfer ratio, move only what the booking needs, and book while it is live.

It is also worth remembering that displayed award space is not always real. Partner integrations can show seats that a program will not actually confirm, a problem we unpack in [phantom award space and why seats vanish](/blog/phantom-award-space-why-seats-vanish). A brand-new partner like Condor is precisely the kind of connection where a displayed seat deserves a second look before you commit points to it.

## The catch, stated plainly

No partnership is pure upside, and an honest read includes the friction:

- **Carrier charges vary by route.** The European routings in particular can add a real cash cost on top of the points, so a cheap-looking award is not always cheap.
- **Premium Economy is not a true premium cabin** on Condor, by many accounts, so do not overpay points for it.
- **Everything is a connection.** With no Condor service to Australia, you are always combining Condor with another carrier, which adds a layer of coordination and its own award-space risk.
- **The Asia network is limited**, so the partnership is most useful for Europe and North America, less so for travelers routing through Bangkok.

None of these are reasons to skip Condor. They are reasons to check the whole picture before you book, rather than reacting to a low headline number.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I earn Qantas Points by flying Condor?

No. This is a redemption-only partnership. You can spend Qantas Points on Condor flights, but you will not earn Qantas Points or status credits for flying them. If earning matters to you, book the cabin you want through a carrier that credits to Qantas, and save Condor for using points rather than accruing them.

### Do I need to live in Australia to use this?

No. Qantas Frequent Flyer is open to members worldwide, and in the United States you can build a Qantas balance by transferring from programs like American Express Membership Rewards, Citi ThankYou, Capital One, and Bilt. Confirm the current transfer ratio for your specific card before moving anything, because ratios and partner lists change.

### Is Condor business class worth the points?

For long-haul on the A330neo, generally yes. The lie-flat Business seat in a 1-2-1 layout with direct aisle access is a genuine business product and reviews are largely positive, and the roughly 100,000-point rate on routes like Bangkok to Frankfurt is competitive for a partner award. Premium Economy is the cabin to scrutinize, since it reads closer to extra-legroom economy than a true premium seat.

### What is the single most common mistake booking a new partner like this?

Transferring points before the seat is confirmed. Award space on a new partner can be volatile and occasionally phantom, and transfers into Qantas do not reverse. Always confirm the exact Condor segment is bookable, in your cabin and dates, then transfer only what that booking requires, then book immediately.
