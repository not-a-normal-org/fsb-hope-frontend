**Air France is selling business class between Europe and New York for 45,000 Flying Blue miles each way, and Capital One's 20 percent transfer bonus brings the cost down to 37,500 Capital One miles.** Both run until November 30, 2026, and the travel window stretches to April 30, 2027. The seat is real and the discount is real. The cash surcharge, the fare rules and the transfer timing are where this one is won or lost.

## Key takeaways

- The 45,000 mile price is business class, each way, on Air France between Europe and New York, so a round trip is 90,000 miles before any bonus.
- Capital One's 20 percent bonus means 37,500 Capital One miles become 45,000 Flying Blue miles, and 75,000 become the full round trip.
- The promo price is the Light fare, which cannot be changed or refunded by anyone below Platinum, and the cash surcharge on top runs into the hundreds of dollars.
- Flying Blue says transferred miles can take one to five days and occasionally up to fifteen, and every transfer is final, so the seat should be confirmed before the miles move.

Flying Blue refreshes a set of discounted awards on the first day of every month and calls them Promo Rewards. Most months the list is unremarkable. This month it contains the one route that transatlantic flyers actually want, at the lowest price the program publishes, and a bank bonus landed on top of it in the same thirty day window. That combination is worth a close look, including the parts the deal posts tend to skip.

## What the promo actually is

The offer is 25 percent off Flying Blue's starting price, which puts business class between Europe and New York at 45,000 miles each way. Work the discount backwards and the usual starting price is 60,000 miles. Flying Blue publishes no award chart, so treat 60,000 as arithmetic from the airline's own 25 percent label rather than a number the program prints.

Two details get lost in the headline. The first is the carrier. The New York business offer is an Air France one, which in practice means the flight runs through Paris. There is no separate Amsterdam to New York business promo this month, although the promo does include a connecting flight within Europe on either end, so an Amsterdam departure reaches the same price by connecting through Paris on Air France metal.

The second is the word "starting". Flying Blue describes the promo figure as the lowest possible price displayed, subject to availability. On a date with no seat in the lowest bucket, the price is simply higher. The promo does not create seats. It discounts the ones already sitting in the cheapest bucket, which is the same bucket that [phantom award space](/blog/phantom-award-space-why-seats-vanish) tends to haunt.

Other US cities appear in this month's list at 18,750 miles in economy. New York is the only one carrying a business price of 45,000.

The Christmas run is the surprise. December 21 through 26 in business, at the cheapest price the program offers, is not what the last week of December normally looks like. If your dates are flexible and you have been waiting for a reason to go, that is the cluster to check first.

## What the Capital One bonus is worth

Capital One transfers to Flying Blue at 1:1 as standard. Through November 30, 2026, a transfer earns 20 percent more, so 1,000 Capital One miles arrive as 1,200 Flying Blue miles.

Run that against the promo price. A 45,000 mile award needs 37,500 Capital One miles. A round trip at 90,000 needs 75,000. Flying Blue takes transfers in increments of 100 above a 1,000 mile minimum, so 37,500 is a clean number you can actually send.

Be careful with the direction of the arithmetic. The bonus divides, it does not multiply: 45,000 divided by 1.2 is 37,500, not 54,000. And the economy promos do not divide neatly at all. An 18,750 mile economy award works out to 15,625 Capital One miles, which is not a multiple of 100, so you would send 15,700 and strand the difference. Any promo price worth transferring for should divide cleanly by 120.

Capital One bonuses to Flying Blue are rare, and 20 percent sits at the low end of the range these bonuses usually land in. If you also hold Amex, Chase, Citi or Bilt points, it is worth checking whether one of those is running something better before you commit. ## The cash is the catch

Miles do not cover taxes and carrier surcharges, and Air France is not shy about either. The amount appears only inside a priced booking, and the airline publishes no figure, so anyone quoting you an exact number before you search is guessing.

What is well established is the shape of it. Reported one way business figures leaving Europe have run from roughly $380 to $490 over the past year, and one traveller documented paying about $455 on a Europe originating promo award in August 2026. Fees also rose during 2026, so older figures are more likely a floor than a quote.

The useful part is the asymmetry. The same cabin leaving New York has been reported at closer to $240. Direction, not mileage, is where the cash difference lives on this route. If you are booking a round trip and have any choice about which half to put on miles, that gap is worth more than the promo discount itself. Our post on [fuel surcharges](/blog/avoid-fuel-surcharges-award-flights) explains why some programs pass these through and others do not.

## Light means light

Since September 2026, Flying Blue reward tickets come in Light, Standard and Flex, and the promo price is always the Light fare. In business, Light means no changes and no refunds. Platinum and Ultimate members can change and refund Light for free. Everyone else is buying a ticket they cannot undo.

The Standard fare is discounted by the same 25 percent and can be changed or refunded for about 70 euros plus any fare difference, which prices the same seat at roughly 56,250 miles. That is 11,250 miles more for the ability to change your mind. On a trip five months out, with deposits or connections attached, that is usually the better buy. Reward tickets also earn no miles and no status credit, whichever fare you choose.

Business Light includes one checked bag but no advance seat selection, which on a transatlantic business ticket is a genuine downgrade from what most people expect.

## The transfer trap

This is the part that quietly costs people the most. Flying Blue's own terms say transferred miles are generally credited within one to five days and may take up to fifteen. Plenty of write ups call Capital One transfers instant. The program itself does not promise that.

Every transfer is also final. There is no route back to Capital One. So the order of operations matters: find the seat, confirm it is pricing at 45,000, and only then move the miles. Speculatively transferring in late November because the bonus is expiring is how people end up holding Flying Blue miles and no award.

## Frequently asked questions

### How many Capital One miles do I need for a 45,000 mile Flying Blue award?

With the 20 percent bonus running through November 30, 2026, you need 37,500 Capital One miles, because 1,000 Capital One miles arrive as 1,200 Flying Blue miles and 45,000 divided by 1.2 is 37,500. A round trip at 90,000 Flying Blue miles needs 75,000 Capital One miles. Flying Blue accepts transfers in increments of 100 above a 1,000 mile minimum, so both figures transfer cleanly without stranding a remainder in your Capital One account.

### Is 45,000 miles each way or for the round trip?

Each way. A round trip in business between Europe and New York is 90,000 Flying Blue miles at the promo price, or 75,000 Capital One miles after the November bonus. The promo price is also described by Flying Blue as a starting price subject to availability, so a given date may well price higher if nothing is left in the cheapest bucket. You can mix a promo award in one direction with a cash fare or a different program in the other.

### Can I change or cancel a Flying Blue promo award?

Generally no. The promo price is the Light fare, and in business Light cannot be changed or refunded unless you hold Platinum or Ultimate status, who can do both free of charge. The Standard fare carries the same 25 percent discount and can be changed or refunded for roughly 70 euros plus any fare difference, which works out to about 56,250 miles for the same seat. If your plans are not firm, the extra 11,250 miles buys real flexibility.

### Does the promo apply to flights from Amsterdam?

Not as a separate Amsterdam to New York offer. The business class promo to New York is an Air France one, so the transatlantic leg runs through Paris. The promo does include a connecting flight within Europe at either end, which means an Amsterdam departure can reach the same 45,000 mile price by connecting through Paris on Air France. Promo prices apply only to Air France and KLM operated flights, never to partner airlines, so a SkyTeam partner routing will not price at this level.
