---BODY---
**Alaska Mileage Plan and HawaiianMiles became Atmos Rewards, and miles became points at one for one.** The two developments worth your attention are later and larger: from January 1, 2027 members choose how they earn points, with different defaults for existing and new members, and during 2027 Bank of America cardholders are due to gain the ability to transfer in.

## Key takeaways

- The conversion itself was clean: one Mileage Plan or HawaiianMiles mile became one Atmos point, and Alaska said at the time that the value of points did not change.
- The repricing came later and separately. The Alaska and Hawaiian award chart was reworked in October 2025, the partner award fee rose to $20 per person each way in July 2026, and cheap economy fares stopped earning anything in August 2026.
- From January 1, 2027, members choose to earn by distance traveled, price paid or segments flown, and can change that choice once per calendar year.
- The defaults differ. Existing members stay on distance unless they act; members joining on or after January 1, 2027 default to price paid.
- Alaska says that during 2027 eligible Bank of America cardholders will be able to transfer points into Atmos, which would give that issuer a transferable-points route for the first time.

Most of the coverage treated this as a renaming exercise, which it was for about a week. The substance arrived afterwards: two announcements about how you earn, and a quieter run of changes that did move what awards cost.

## What the rebrand actually did

Alaska launched Atmos Rewards on August 20, 2025, combining Mileage Plan and Hawaiian's HawaiianMiles. Miles became "points" and elite-qualifying miles became "status points".

The transition ran in two stages. Mileage Plan members became Atmos members immediately and kept their account numbers. HawaiianMiles members and their balances moved over on October 1, 2025, and those who had not already linked their accounts were issued a new number. One mile became one point either way.

Worth being precise about one line that gets repeated. Alaska said at the transition that the value of points did not change, and that was true of the conversion itself. It was not a promise about the future, and the program has repriced since, including changes to its own award chart and to what discounted fares earn. Treat it as a statement about August 2025 rather than a standing guarantee.

What did carry across is the machinery that made the program worth holding: award charts published by region for partner redemptions, plus a separate chart for Alaska and Hawaiian flights, and membership of oneworld, which Hawaiian joined on April 23, 2026. Those charts are still the reason award travelers pay attention, with Alaska's own caveat that published figures are starting rates and actual requirements vary.

## The repricing nobody covered

The rename was cosmetic. The year around it was not, and these are the changes that actually reached people booking awards.

**The Alaska and Hawaiian chart was rebuilt in October 2025.** Three separate charts collapsed into one distance-banded chart for the airlines' own metal. Short Hawaii routes and inter-island awards got cheaper; long-haul Hawaii first class went from a flat 40,000 to 60,000, a straightforward 50% devaluation if that was your redemption. Own-metal awards now also move with demand, so those chart numbers are floors rather than prices. Partner awards still run on the fixed distance charts.

**The partner award booking fee doubled.** From July 1, 2026 it is $20 per person each way, up from $12.50, and nonrefundable. On a family of four round-trip that is $160 before taxes.

**Cheap economy stopped earning.** Saver fares booked from June 11, 2026 and flown from August 1, 2026 earn no points and no status points at all, down from 30% of distance, though they still count toward Million Miler.

**And partner awards have been pricing above chart since March 2026.** Travelers found connecting partner itineraries quoted far above the published distance band, with Europe and Asia routings roughly doubling. Alaska told two outlets it was a technical issue affecting some partner connections, since resolved, with no changes to the charts. That is where the story stops being tidy: readers on both outlets reported the same behaviour weeks later, and as recently as October 2026 a transatlantic business award was reported quoted at 175,000 points against a 45,000 chart price with saver space showing. Either the fix is partial or above-chart partner pricing is now normal. Anyone telling you the charts hold without qualification has not checked.

## The choice arriving on January 1

This is the genuinely unusual part. From January 1, 2027, Atmos members select how they earn points and status points on flights:

- **Distance traveled**, the model Alaska has been known for and one of the last of its kind among large US programs. Note that it becomes a flat one point per mile: the 125% to 200% class-of-service multipliers that premium fares earn today do not survive into the distance option.
- **Price paid**, the revenue-based model most of the industry moved to years ago.
- **Segments flown**, a flat award per segment, which rewards frequency rather than distance or spend.

Members could make the selection from October 1, 2026 for travel on or after January 1, 2027, and can change it once per calendar year.

Offering the choice at all is a real differentiator. A long-haul leisure flyer buying cheap tickets across oceans wants distance. Somebody buying expensive short hops wants price. A commuter doing many short segments wants segments. Historically a program picked one and you lived with it.

The choice also governs more than Alaska's own flights: it covers partner flights booked through Alaska or Hawaiian, and American Airlines flights however they were booked.

One catch undercuts the generosity. Alaska promised a large increase to partner earning, international business going from 125% to 250%, arriving with the earning choice. Reporting in October 2026 indicates that boost is tied to selecting **price paid**, meaning the distance option, the one existing members keep by default, does not get it. If that holds, a premium-fare flyer who stays on distance loses the class-of-service multipliers *and* misses the partner boost. Alaska has not confirmed or denied the gating, so treat the 250% figure as unlaunched and conditional.

## The defaults are the part to read twice

A choice is only as good as what happens when nobody makes it, and here the defaults diverge.

**Existing members** who do nothing continue earning by **distance**. That is the generous outcome, and it protects people who built balances under the old model.

**New members joining on or after January 1, 2027** default to **price paid**.

That second default is the quiet shift. Distance-based earning has been one of the clearest reasons to credit flights to Alaska rather than elsewhere, and new members will arrive on the revenue-based model unless they go and change it. Nothing is taken from anyone. The program simply stops handing its most distinctive feature to newcomers automatically.

If you join, or you have family joining, make the selection deliberately rather than inheriting it.

## The Bank of America bridge

The second announcement matters more to points holders than anything above. Alaska has said that during 2027 it plans to let customers with eligible Bank of America card products transfer points into Atmos Rewards, and that it intends to add arrangements with other financial institutions over time.

Bank of America's own travel currency has competed on cash-back-style redemptions, fixed at roughly a cent toward travel, while Chase, American Express, Capital One and Citi built the transferable-points ecosystem that makes premium-cabin award travel work. One precision, since it trips people up: Bank of America has issued the Alaska and Atmos co-branded cards for years, and those earn Atmos points directly. What it has never had is an airline or hotel transfer partner for its *own* bank points. A route from Bank of America into Atmos would change that, and it would do it through a program with a genuinely strong partner chart rather than a weak one.

Two cautions, both important. First, the ratio has not been announced, and ratio is everything: a partner at 1:0.7 is a very different proposition from one at 1:1, as we showed when working through [whether a transfer bonus is worth taking](/blog/are-transfer-bonuses-worth-it). Second, "during 2027" is not a date, and Alaska's own wording is that it plans to. Treat it as a direction of travel, not something to choose a credit card over today.

Meanwhile the existing ways in are wider than usually reported. Alongside the co-branded cards, Atmos points can be reached from Bilt Rewards at one for one, Marriott Bonvoy at three to one, IHG One Rewards and Choice Privileges at five to one, and Diners Club Club Rewards at one for one. The hotel ratios are poor enough to be a way to use up an orphaned balance rather than a strategy, and watch the direction: Atmos also transfers *out* to several hotel programs on better terms, and those outbound ratios get quoted as if they were inbound.

## What this means for a redeemer

No deadline is bearing down on you, so there is nothing to panic-transfer. But the honest version is not "nothing changed."

If you redeem on Alaska or Hawaiian metal, the October 2025 chart is your reality now. If you redeem on partners, budget the $20 fee per person each way and **price the itinerary before you transfer anything**, because the above-chart partner quotes have not been convincingly put to bed. If you earn on cheap fares, you have been earning nothing on them since August.

What changes next is where to credit flights, and potentially which cards are worth holding in 2027. Both are earning decisions. Neither requires you to move points now, and moving points into any airline program before you have a confirmed booking remains the mistake it has always been, as [the transfer partners guide](/blog/transfer-partners-explained-guide) sets out. That has never applied more sharply than to a program whose partner engine is quoting prices its own chart does not explain.

Alaska's partner charts are still the reason to care, including for routes to Asia where its pricing has historically undercut the operating carrier's own program. Our guide to [business class to Tokyo with points](/blog/business-class-to-tokyo-with-points) covers how that comparison works, so confirm the live price on the day.

## Frequently asked questions

### Did my Alaska miles change value when they became Atmos points?

The conversion was one for one. One Mileage Plan mile and one HawaiianMiles mile each became one Atmos Rewards point, Mileage Plan members kept their account numbers, and Alaska said at the time that the value of points did not change. Read that as a statement about the transition rather than a standing guarantee, because the program has repriced since, including changes to its own award chart and to what discounted fares earn.

### What changes on January 1, 2027?

Members choose how they earn points and status points on flights: by distance traveled, by price paid, or by segments flown. The selection opened on October 1, 2026 for flights departing on or after January 1, 2027, and members can change it once per calendar year. Existing members who make no selection continue earning by distance, while members who join on or after January 1, 2027 default to earning by price paid. The choice also applies to partner flights booked through Alaska or Hawaiian.

### Will Bank of America points transfer to Atmos Rewards?

Alaska has said it plans to enable transfers into Atmos Rewards from eligible Bank of America card products during 2027, and that it expects to add similar arrangements with other financial institutions over time. The transfer ratio, the eligible cards and the exact timing have not been published, and neither Alaska nor Bank of America has issued anything further. Until they do, treat it as a stated intention rather than a feature, and do not pick a credit card today on the strength of it.

### Did Atmos award prices go up?

Some did, and one question is still open. The Alaska and Hawaiian own-metal chart was rebuilt on October 1, 2025: short Hawaii routes and inter-island awards got cheaper, while long-haul Hawaii first class went from a flat 40,000 to 60,000. The partner award booking fee rose to $20 per person each way on July 1, 2026. The published partner distance charts themselves have not been revised, but since March 2026 travelers have repeatedly reported connecting partner itineraries quoted well above the chart price. Alaska described that as a resolved technical issue; reports of the same behaviour have continued since. Price your specific itinerary before you transfer points into the program.
