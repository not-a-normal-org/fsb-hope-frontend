**Until November 30, 2026, Miles & More is adding up to 50% bonus miles to a member's first Bundle&Go purchase, which brings the largest bundle to 225,000 miles for $3,160, around 1.4 cents a mile.** That is cheap for Lufthansa miles. It is worth it for one thing: Lufthansa First Class, which partner programs have not been able to book since June 1, 2026. For business class, the points you already hold usually book a comparable seat for far less cash.

## Key takeaways

- Miles & More is adding up to 50% bonus miles to a first Bundle&Go purchase until November 30, 2026, and the bonus is personalized, so check the figure your own account shows before you pay.
- With the full bonus, the X-Large bundle prices a Miles & More mile at around 1.4 cents in US dollars, and without a bonus, buying miles rarely makes sense.
- Since June 1, 2026, Lufthansa has released no First Class award seats to partner programs, so Miles & More is now the only practical way to book it with miles.
- For business class, Miles & More awards from the United States carry around $1,500 each way in taxes and surcharges, against roughly $5 to $120 through United or Aeroplan, so transferring the bank points you already hold usually wins.

The Lufthansa Group's program has sold miles through Bundle&Go, a store run by the loyalty company Points, for several years. For most of that time a bonus sale was easy to ignore from the United States: none of Amex, Chase, Citi or Capital One transfers to Miles & More, and the Lufthansa seats worth having could be booked through partner programs instead.

That changed this year. With Lufthansa First gone from partner programs, buying Miles & More miles has become the most direct way for most American travelers to redeem for it, and a bonus sale is when those miles cost least.

## What the offer actually is

Bundle&Go sells miles in four packages, each with extras attached. With the full 50% bonus, the packages work out like this in US dollars, at the time of writing:

- **Small:** 7,500 miles for $150, about 2 cents a mile.
- **Medium:** 75,000 miles and one Lufthansa Business Lounge voucher for $1,250, about 1.67 cents.
- **Large:** 150,000 miles, 100 status Points and two lounge vouchers for $2,200, about 1.47 cents.
- **X-Large:** 225,000 miles, 150 status Points and three lounge vouchers for $3,160, about 1.4 cents.

Members in Europe are quoted the same four packages in euros: €125, €1,050, €1,850 and €2,650, between 1.18 and 1.67 euro cents a mile.

The bonus is the deal. Without it, a mile from the cheapest package costs around 1.77 euro cents, and at that price buying almost never beats paying cash for the seat.

The rules matter as much as the price:

- The bonus applies to your first bundle only, and Miles & More describes it as available to selected members, at up to 50%. Some accounts see less, and some see none. October's sale was open to every member, so if you bought a bundle then, this one is not for you.
- You can buy one bundle per transaction, and up to 300,000 miles per calendar year through Bundle&Go.
- Purchases are final. There are no refunds.
- Miles are due within five business days, though in practice they often arrive within hours.

## What a bought mile is worth

A Miles & More mile is only worth what it saves you on a specific flight, and the program's pricing makes that harder to judge than it used to be. Since June 2025, awards on Lufthansa, SWISS and Austrian flights have been priced dynamically, so the miles move with the cash fare instead of sitting on a chart.

Two things follow. There is no fixed number to plan around: a First Class seat to Europe can cost around 100,000 miles on one date and more than 200,000 on another. And the taxes and carrier surcharges sit on top of the miles, which on long-haul Lufthansa Group flights are large.

That is why buying miles to have them ready is the wrong move here. Search the exact flight, see the miles and the cash together, and buy only when the total beats paying for the seat outright.

## The one cabin your bank points can no longer reach

Lufthansa First Class has always been hard to book with partner miles. For years Lufthansa released First Class award seats to Star Alliance partners only in roughly the last two weeks before departure, and in early 2024 that window shrank to a few days. Since June 1, 2026, according to One Mile at a Time, Live and Let's Fly, The Points Guy and award-tracking data, it has released none at all. Lufthansa never announced the change, and there has been no sign of it reversing.

Miles & More members still see First Class, up to 361 days before departure. That makes this program the only practical way to book Lufthansa First with miles, and a bonus sale the cheapest way to get those miles if you do not already have them.

The numbers are not small. In award trackers' October data, Lufthansa First from the United States to Europe ran from around 100,000 miles one way on itineraries with a connection to roughly 165,000 to 206,000 on nonstops, plus about $1,550 to $1,610 in taxes and surcharges. At around 1.4 cents a mile, an X-Large bundle's 225,000 miles covers one nonstop seat, or two connecting seats on a cheaper date, with the cash due on top each time.

Whether that beats the cash fare depends entirely on the date. On a good one, the miles and surcharge together come to a fraction of what Lufthansa charges for the same seat. On an expensive one, the dynamic price can erase most of the saving.

## Why business class is usually cheaper with the points you already hold

For business class, the comparison flips. Partner programs still show Lufthansa business award seats, mostly on itineraries with a connection, so the points you already hold can usually reach them: Amex Membership Rewards, Chase Ultimate Rewards, Capital One and Bilt all transfer to Air Canada Aeroplan, and Chase and Bilt transfer to United MileagePlus.

The difference is the cash. On business class from the United States to Europe, Miles & More awards were carrying around $1,450 to $1,555 one way in taxes and carrier surcharges in award trackers' October data. One Atlanta to Amsterdam itinerary on Lufthansa showed the gap plainly: 99,232 Miles & More miles plus $1,547.80, against 75,000 Aeroplan points plus $119.61, or 80,000 United miles plus $5.60. Even with a cheaper return from Europe, the round-trip gap typically runs past $2,000 per person, which is the subject of [our guide to avoiding fuel surcharges](/blog/avoid-fuel-surcharges-award-flights).

The honest caveat runs the other way. Nonstop Lufthansa business flights often show only in Miles & More, while partners mostly see itineraries with a connection. If the nonstop on your date is the seat you want, that is when buying miles can still make sense for business, as long as the seat is in front of you before you pay.

## Status Points and lounge vouchers

Since September, the two larger bundles also carry Miles & More status Points: 100 with the Large and 150 with the X-Large. They are Points, not Qualifying Points, and that distinction decides what they are worth. Frequent Traveller status takes 650 Points and 325 Qualifying Points in a calendar year, and Senator takes 2,000 and 1,000, so a bundle can top up the first figure but never supply the second. They do not count toward HON Circle at all. For a US traveler already close to Frequent Traveller on flown Lufthansa trips, they can help close the gap. For anyone else, they are a footnote.

The lounge vouchers, one to three depending on the bundle, open Lufthansa Business Lounges. Like the status Points, they arrive later than the miles, by the seventh of the month after you buy. They are useful if you are already flying through Frankfurt or Munich in economy, and of no use if you are flying business, which includes the lounge anyway.

## The rules that catch people

Three things go wrong more often than the price does.

**The bonus you see is the bonus you get.** The offer is personalized and described as up to 50%. Check the miles shown for each bundle in your own account at checkout, not the headline.

**Miles do not always arrive instantly, and dynamic prices do not wait.** The terms allow up to five business days. If the award is priced dynamically, the seat can reprice or disappear in that gap, which is the same risk described in [why award seats vanish](/blog/phantom-award-space-why-seats-vanish). If the seat you want is the last one on the date, accept that it may be gone when the miles land.

**Bought miles expire like any others.** Miles & More miles last 36 months unless you hold status or an eligible Miles & More credit card. In the United States that includes the Barclays Miles & More World Elite Mastercard, as long as you have held it for three months and use it at least once a month. Buy for a trip you can book now, not for one you might take in 2028.

Underneath all three is the rule that applies to every transfer and every purchase: search first, buy second, as [the transfer partners guide](/blog/transfer-partners-explained-guide) sets out.

## Frequently asked questions

### How much does it cost to buy Miles & More miles with the 50% bonus?

With the full 50% bonus, at the time of writing, US-dollar accounts pay $150 for 7,500 miles, $1,250 for 75,000, $2,200 for 150,000 and $3,160 for 225,000, which ranges from about 2 cents a mile down to about 1.4 cents. Members in Europe pay €125, €1,050, €1,850 and €2,650 for the same packages. The bonus applies to your first bundle only, runs until November 30, 2026, and is personalized, so the miles shown in your own account are what you will actually receive.

### Can I book Lufthansa First Class with United or Aeroplan miles?

Not in practice at the moment. For years Lufthansa released First Class award seats to Star Alliance partners only shortly before departure, but since June 1, 2026, loyalty news outlets and award trackers report that it has released none, so United MileagePlus, Air Canada Aeroplan and Avianca LifeMiles show no Lufthansa First space. Lufthansa never announced the change. Miles & More members can still book it, up to 361 days before departure, at dynamically priced mileage with carrier surcharges on top.

### Do bought Miles & More miles expire?

Yes. Miles bought through Bundle&Go follow the program's normal rule and expire after 36 months unless you hold Miles & More status or an eligible Miles & More credit card, which in the United States includes the Barclays Miles & More World Elite Mastercard if you use it every month. Buy for a trip you can book now rather than building a balance for later.

### Does every member get the 50% bonus?

No. Miles & More describes the offer as available to selected members and applies it only to a first bundle purchase, and the bonus is shown as up to 50%, so some accounts see a smaller bonus or none, and anyone who already bought a bundle, including in October's sale, does not qualify. Log in and check the miles listed for each bundle before you buy, because the figure in your account is the one that applies.
